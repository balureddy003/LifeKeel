# Organic positioning research: how LifeKeel earns attention without paid ads (October 2026)

Research date: 24 October 2026. Method: web search plus reading primary pages where they would open.
This builds on [market-positioning-october-2026.md](market-positioning-october-2026.md), which maps
competitors and product positioning. This report covers a different question: **what to say, where, and
in what form, to earn organic attention in the UK**, and how much of the evidence can be trusted.

Evidence grades: **A** primary or official page read; **B** reputable secondary report, figures as
reported; **C** vendor or trade blog, treat as directional; **?** could not verify.

## 1. Summary

1. The organic story is **"knowing where things are and what to do next"**, not money management. The
   strongest public numbers are about preparedness and admin, not wealth.
2. A ready-made demand signal exists: people already build "death binders" and "if I die" folders by
   hand. LifeKeel is the living, kept-current version, and a printable starter sheet can meet that
   demand directly.
3. Entrants in this space launch with **research and partners**, not posts (Empathy UK: a report plus an
   insurer partner; Everplans: a content library, then adviser partnerships; Farewill: a brand story and
   charity partnerships).
4. A caution from the evidence: in one YouGov survey, nearly two thirds of bereaved administrators said
   they would rather speak to a person than use an app. Position as the **prepared file that makes the
   human conversation easy**, not as a replacement for people.
5. Regulatory fit holds: purely factual content with no persuasion is generally outside the FCA
   "invitation or inducement" test. That supports the information-only line, but needs a one-time
   professional review for each format that gets close to products.

- **Reach by format:** short video (Reels) outperforms static posts on Instagram; LinkedIn company-page reach is reported falling, with documents and carousels doing best; Facebook pages are weakest. - **AI search ("GEO"):** advice from vendors says to publish quotable facts with sources, and that brand mentions elsewhere correlate with being cited more than backlinks do. AI referrals were reported at about 1.7% of small-site visits. [Beancount guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/16/generative-engine-optimization-geo-small-business-guide), [MEAN CEO](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=11358). Grade C.
- **Communities:** r/UKPersonalFinance asks authors to paste their content into the post rather than send readers to a site; MoneySavingExpert's forum describes itself as free of selling; r/HENRYUK suggests a test of whether a post would also help your competitors. ## 3. What the evidence says about positioning

**Say:** LifeKeel keeps the family file current, so a partner can find what is on file and what to do next.
This matches the strongest public numbers (unsure what to do, 7+ organisations to contact, almost half
of wills out of date).

**Avoid:**
- "AI financial advisor", "better policy", "buy", "switch": regulated territory and a fight with
  better-funded players.
- Competing with Empathy on bereavement logistics. LifeKeel is the "before", Empathy is the "after".
- Treating the survey figures as LifeKeel claims. Use them only when they are in the claims registry with
  source and date.

**Tone:** the audience may be anxious or recently bereaved (the FCA also asks firms to consider vulnerable
audiences). Calm, practical, no fear or urgency, as the copy guard already enforces.

**Tension to manage:** the "prefer a person" finding does not contradict the product if the output is a
tidy handover (the sheet, the checklist) that makes talking to a solicitor, adviser or family easier.

## 4. Innovative organic approaches, ranked

All free to run. "Intent" points to the build specs in `docs/intents/`.

1. **A living "if something happened" file.** Meet the existing death-binder habit with a free
   printable starter sheet and an interactive version in the product. UK wording: "what to do when
   someone dies checklist", "Tell Us Once", "where are the papers". Intent 17.
2. **Search-led guides built from real questions.** Use Search Console and search suggestions to pick
   topics, then publish plain, sourced answers (the guides and revival job exist; the read-back does not).
   Grade of demand evidence: none yet, so this must be measured.
3. **Calendar moments, done carefully.** Dying Matters Week, the Pensions Dashboard launch, tax year end.
   The calendar format is held for professional review.
4. **A sourced "UK family readiness" digest**, refreshed each quarter, built only from published
   figures with links. This is the cheap version of the research-led launch Empathy used, and the
   quotable, cited format AI search favours. No LifeKeel user data.
5. **An answer desk** for public questions, drafted by Keel and posted by a person, respecting each
   community's rules. Intent 18.
6. **Partnerships that already carry trust:** charities and hospices (the Farewill pattern), employers'
   benefit teams, advisers who may offer an informational tool (the Everplans pattern). Slow, but the
   likeliest way to reach people who would rather speak to a person.
7. **Short video and carousels** (Reels, LinkedIn documents). Test, do not assume; the data is vendor-grade.
8. **Founder story:** why this exists, in the founder's voice. Pairs with 5 and 6.
