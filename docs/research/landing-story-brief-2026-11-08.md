# LifeKeel landing pages: the story, and a review of every page

Date: 2026-11-08. For the owner. Status: proposal. Nothing on the site has changed.

## In short

1. **The landing page tells a money-runway story. The product now delivers a bigger one.**
   The page opens with "One money question. One number. Both of you." and spends most of its
   length on briefs, figures and months. The product the families on staging actually use is a
   shared **family list** that every adult confirms, with first steps if something happens.
   The words "family list" appear nowhere on the home page.
2. **The strongest emotional material is buried.** "When something happens, your family has
   somewhere to start" is section 6 of 10. The page opens with homework, not with a feeling.
3. **Plain English has drifted.** The page says "brief", "runway", "roster", "papers on file".
   The tested, plain nouns are "family list" and "the family".
4. **Good bones to keep.** The Before / During / After frame, "six things Keel does not do",
   the honest sample-household labels, and the claims registry that stops the page promising
   more than the product does.
5. **Recommended story:** *the day someone else has to find everything.* Not fear of what
   happens, but the quiet relief of a family that knows where to look. Told through ordinary
   objects, calm competence and a shared check, in that order.

## The story

**One line.** LifeKeel is the list your family will be glad exists on the day someone needs it.

**The idea we are borrowing, in one phrase.** *Leave them a map, not a mystery.*

**Three acts, one for each moment the product already names.**

| Act | Moment | Feeling | What the product does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before | An ordinary Tuesday. One person holds it all in their head. | Recognition, a little guilt, mostly "I should" | Add a car, a policy, a school date once. See what is coming up. |
| The pause | Both adults look at the same list. | Relief. "Oh, you know that too." | Each adult confirms. Change anything and everyone looks again. |
| During and after | A bad week. Someone has to find things. | Calm. "We know where to start." | Steps from what is already on file, who to call, who can open what. |

**Who it is for.** One adult or two. Step-families, house-shares, lone parents, a family that
has moved. All of these were run through staging as fictional households (40 of them, 136 of
137 expectations met on the latest run). The one miss: a lodger who joins sees the whole list.
Do not promise "a household of any kind" until that has a limited view.

**Tone.** Warm, a little wry, British, calm. Mission control, not a charity appeal.

## What the pages say today

I read the home page in full (hero and every section), the FAQ, the claims registry and the
story board. I only skimmed the headings of the other public pages. Not reviewed: blog posts,
legal and privacy pages, the `.si` site (does it need its own language version?), and the first-run welcome slides in detail.

### Draft copy

Every sentence below was scored with the repo's readability scorer. Worst sentence: grade 8.4
(limit 9). Nothing here is final. Each line must map to a claim in `claims.ts` before it ships.

**1. Hero**
- Eyebrow: A shared family list
- Headline: **The day someone needs it, it is already there.**
- Under it: LifeKeel keeps what your family would look for on one list. Policies, cars, school
  dates and who to call. Every adult has seen it.
- Buttons: Start your family's list · See how it works
- Under the buttons: Free to start. No card needed. We earn no commissions. We never move your
  money. This is information, not advice.

Other headlines tried: *Leave them a map, not a mystery.* (2.3) · *One list your whole family
can find.* (4.0) · *Could the other adult find everything?* (8.4, a question, so a little
sharper) · *Everything your family would look for, on one list you have both seen.* (5.8).

**2. Three ordinary moments** (objects, not explanations)
- *The MOT ran out last week and nobody noticed.* Add the car once. LifeKeel shows the MOT and
  renewal dates, and what is coming up next.
- *Where is the life policy, and who do we ring?* Policies, who holds them, who they cover and
  who to call, all in one place.
- *It has been a bad week. Where do we start?* Pick a person and see steps that fit what you
  have added. In an emergency, call 999.

**3. Both of you have looked**
- Both of you have looked. That is the point.
- Each adult confirms they have seen the list. If anything changes, everyone looks again.
  Nobody works from an old version.
- You can see who has confirmed and who has not.
- Someone you care for does not need to confirm. You can say so.
- You decide who can change the list. Children are never asked to confirm.

**5. Made for real families**
- One adult or two. A step-family or a house-share. A family on the move.
- LifeKeel works the way your household does. *(Hold until a limited view for lodgers and
  helpers exists. See the guardrails.)*

**6. What it never does, and what it gives**
- It is free to start. Here is what it will never do.
- It never earns a commission. It never moves your money. It never tells you what to buy.
- Your family details are not sold.

**7. Three steps**
- Add what you have. A car, a policy, a school date.
- Both adults look. Each one confirms.
- Find it when it matters. Open the list, or the first steps.

**9. Close**
- Start with one car or one policy. Add the rest when you are ready.

## Guardrails

- **Every claim needs backing.** The registry in `components/landing/claims.ts` has no entry for
  the family list, the shared confirmation, first steps from what is on file, the edit
  permission, the monthly note or children and school dates. Each needs a `live` claim with an
  evidence file before it appears on the page, and the registry tests enforce that.
- **No regulated advice.** Facts and dates only. No "you should", no named providers, no product
  picks. The sample household must not read as a nudge ("Income protection is not on file").
- **Plain English.** Grade 9 or lower, no jargon. Use "family list" and "the family", not
  "brief", "roster" or "runway" in the headline copy.
- **Honest limits.** A lodger who joins sees everything today, so do not say "any household".
  Only the warning exists. The digest is monthly, not a live alert.
- **No invented proof.** No testimonials, counts or "families trust us" without real data. The
  sample household stays labelled made-up. Use fictional vignettes, labelled.
- **Fear and distress.** Keep the hero free of death imagery. The bad-week scene is calm and
  practical. Check the advertising code before any paid campaign.
- **Respect.** Include the real range of UK households in sample imagery and names.
- **Safeguarding.** Be careful what the page says about leaving and removing people.

## How we would know it works

1. **Headline test** with the existing sign-up events (`landing_cta_click`): the current
   headline against the new one, same traffic, same week.
2. **Five families** in the usability kit, reading the page cold: what do they think LifeKeel is,
   after ten seconds? Success is "a list my family can find", not "a calculator".
3. **Reading and access checks:** grade 9 on every sentence, 48px targets on all landing controls
   (proposed in #433, not yet merged), a screen-reader pass.
4. **A qualified reviewer** for claims and advertising rules, and a check on whether the `.si` site needs its own language version.
